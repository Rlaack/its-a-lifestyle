Privacy policy

Last updated November 21, 2026

The short version. Your plans, chats and neighbors’ names are encrypted on your phone before they leave it. Our server passes them along but can’t read them. We don’t sell data, show ads, use analytics or track you across apps.

Who we are

It’s a Lifestyle is an app for neighbors to plan things together and chat. It’s made by Cyber Affinity LLC (“we”). The app is open source (AGPL-3.0). Its code will be published so anyone can check what it does.

What stays on your phone

Your neighborhoods, plans, chats, challenge scores, the names you and your neighbors chose, and any cover picture you add are stored on your phone, encrypted with a key kept in your phone’s secure keychain. Your encryption keys never leave your phone.

What our server holds

To deliver messages, a small server (“the relay”) keeps:

  • A random account ID for each phone. It isn’t linked to your name or email. If you subscribe, it’s linked to a purchase only through the hashed ID below. We never ask for an email.
  • Public encryption keys, so other phones can start encrypted conversations with you.
  • Encrypted messages waiting for delivery. Each is deleted as soon as your phone confirms it arrived, or after 30 days if it never does.
  • Neighborhoods, in encrypted form. The relay stores a fingerprint of the invite code (not the code itself), the neighborhood’s name and members’ names encrypted so only people with the code can read them, which account IDs are members, and which accounts were removed.
  • Photos and voice messages you send, encrypted. Before a photo leaves your phone, its hidden details (like the location where it was taken) are removed and it’s encrypted with its own key, which travels only inside the encrypted message. The relay stores the scrambled file under a random ID and deletes it after 30 days, or after about a year for a neighborhood’s cover photo.
  • Your recovery backup, only if you turn it on. It’s encrypted on your phone with a key made from your recovery code, which we never see. We store the scrambled copy (no photos or files) and delete it if you turn the backup off or delete your account.
  • Your subscription, only if you buy one. We store a keyed hash of Apple’s purchase ID, its expiration date, whether it’s a test purchase, your random account ID, and which neighborhoods it covers. We never receive your Apple ID, name, email or payment details.
  • Your linked devices, if you add any. Each has its own number, public keys, the date it was added and a hashed login. Your account’s private identity key reaches a new device only inside an encrypted hand-off that the relay can’t read.
  • A push notification token from Apple, so it can wake your phone. Notifications only ever say “You have something new.”
  • A scrambled copy (a hash) of a private access key your phone shares only with people you talk to. It lets them send you messages without telling the relay who they are.
  • Your phone number, only if you add one so neighbors can find you by number. We store a keyed hash of it, not the number. When you look someone up by number, the numbers are compared the same way and not kept.

What our server can’t see

The content of anything you send: messages, plans, RSVPs, sign-ups, photos, addresses you type into a plan, or anyone’s name. It also can’t see who sent most messages. The app uses Signal’s “sealed sender”, so the relay usually knows only who a message is for.

What our server can see

We’d rather be plain about this:

  • Which account a message is for, when it was sent and roughly how big it is.
  • The sender of the first message you send to someone new. After that first exchange, the sender is hidden.
  • Your IP address while your phone is connected. We use it only to slow down abuse (rate limits) and don’t store it. Our hosting provider (Railway) keeps standard network logs, which can include IP addresses.
  • Which account IDs are in the same neighborhood.

Other companies involved

  • Railway hosts the relay.
  • Apple processes subscription payments and tells the relay when one renews, ends or is refunded. Apple delivers push notifications. When you type an address into a plan, your phone asks Apple Maps to look it up.

No one else receives data from the app. There are no advertising, analytics or crash-reporting services in it.

Permissions the app asks for

  • Camera: only to scan a neighbor’s QR code.
  • Photos: only for photos you choose to send in a chat or set as a neighborhood’s cover picture.
  • Calendar: only if you ask to copy a plan into your calendar.
  • Location: only if you choose to share where you are in a chat. It’s rounded to about 1 km, encrypted, and never tracked in the background.
  • Microphone: only while you record a voice message.
  • Notifications: to tell you something new arrived.
  • Face ID or passcode: if you turn on the app lock. Your phone checks it and we never see it.

Crash reports

If the app closes unexpectedly, it asks you the next time you open it whether to send a crash report. The report holds only the error, where in the app’s code it happened, and the app and iOS versions. It isn’t linked to your account, has no names or messages, and is deleted after 90 days. If you choose not to send it, it’s deleted from your phone.

Deleting your data

In the app, go to Me → Delete my account. This erases your account, keys, phone number hash, push token, memberships and waiting messages from the relay right away, and wipes the app’s data on your phone. Copies of plans and messages already delivered to your neighbors’ phones stay on their phones, as with any message you send.

Closed accounts

If we close an account for breaking the community guidelines, we delete it as above and keep only: the account ID, the date and our reason, scrambled copies (hashes) of its login tokens so its phone can be told why it stopped working, and a keyed hash of its phone number, if it had one, so that number can’t be used again.

Requests from authorities

We can only hand over what the relay holds, listed above. We can’t produce message content because we don’t have the keys.

Security

The encryption uses libsignal, the open-source library behind the Signal app. Our use of it has not yet been independently audited. We’ll update this page when it has.

Age

The app is for adults 18 and older. We don’t knowingly allow accounts for anyone younger, and we close them if we learn about one.

Changes

If this policy changes, we’ll update this page and the date at the top. Big changes will also be announced in the app.
